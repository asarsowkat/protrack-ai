Step by step, by role

What each person does in ProTrack, in the order they do it, and what they are accountable for.

ProTrack works on one principle: every number has exactly one owner. Nobody overwrites anybody. If two people hold different figures, the system shows the difference instead of hiding it.

The golden ruleConstruction progress comes only from daily reports. Design and procurement progress comes only from the weekly update. Price comes only from P6. Cost comes only from the Master Manpower Costing Sheet. No screen lets one of these overwrite another.

ForemanRecords what his crews actually did, every working day

Every day, end of shift, 5 minutes

You are the source of every number in the system. If the crew and the quantity are right, everything above you is right.

Your daily routine

  1. Sign in on your phone. You see only your own project.
  2. Open New report. The date and project are already filled in. Choose your name as foreman if it isn't set.
  3. Pick the work location — the area of the site you worked in, for example "380kV GIS Building" or "Section 14, PI-17 to PI-18".
  4. Pick the work type — Civil, Electrical, Mechanical and so on. Only the types available in that location appear.
  5. Pick the WBS, then the activity. By this point the list is three or four activities, not the whole schedule.
  6. Enter the quantity executed today in the activity's own unit: m³, tonnes, metres, towers.
  7. Enter the crew that produced it — trade, number of workers, regular hours, overtime hours. The usual crew is pre-filled; change the numbers that differ.
  8. Add subcontractor crews if any worked with you: company, trade, workers, hours, and the quantity they executed. Their quantity is part of your total.
  9. Add equipment — crane, excavator, boom truck — with units and working hours.
  10. Add another activity for every other activity your crews touched today. Each gets its own location, quantity and crew.
  11. Check the productivity panel on the right. If it is red, look at it before submitting: usually the crew is right and the quantity was mis-measured, or the crew was waiting.
  12. Write a remark if anything held you up: no access, no material, crane breakdown, rain, client instruction. Managers read these.
  13. Submit. It goes to your site engineer for review, then to your site manager for approval. A clock at the top of the form records how long the report took.

Rules that matter

  • If a worker split the day between two activities, record him on both with the hours he spent on each, for example 4 and 4.
  • Never inflate a quantity to make the index look good. The surveyor's measurement will correct it and your approval rate drops.
  • Record overtime honestly. Overtime is costed at 1.5 times and shows up in your scorecard as a separate line, not as bad productivity.
  • If you cannot finish the report, Save draft. A draft counts for nothing until submitted.
What you are measured onProductivity index, overtime share, rework (reports returned or rejected) and approval rate — combined into your monthly score in Performance → Foremen. Consistent reporting matters as much as fast work.

Site engineerReviews every foreman's report for accuracy

Daily, plus a weekly look at productivity

You either enter the reports yourself or check what the foremen entered. You can edit any report until the project manager approves it.

Daily

  1. Review what your foremen submitted: Daily reports → Submitted → open → Mark as reviewed, or return with comments. You see the button only on reports from foremen assigned to you; it then goes to the site manager assigned to you on that project.
  2. Check the drafts in Daily reports, filtered to Draft. Nothing should stay in draft overnight.
  3. Verify the quantity against the survey or the measurement sheet before submitting. This is the single check that protects every number above you.
  4. Verify the crew against the gate count or attendance. The resources total on the right of the form should reconcile.
  5. Submit, or correct and submit. Edits are logged with your name.
  6. Deal with returned reports the same day. A returned report carries the manager's comment at the top of the audit trail.

Weekly

  • Open Reports → Planned vs actual productivity for your project and look at the activities below 90%.
  • Open Reports → Manpower histogram, set the trade filter, and compare the crew you have against the forecast. Ask for mobilisation early rather than late.
  • Look at Executive summary → Project view for your project: bottlenecks, underperforming activities and foreman performance in one screen.
  • Ask RASA: "which activity is underperforming and why", "what manpower is needed to finish on time".
What you cannot doYou cannot approve your own reports, change the baseline, or change design and procurement progress. That separation is deliberate.

Site managerApproves every reviewed daily report, the same day

Every evening, and before site meetings

The foreman submits, the site engineer reviews, and you approve. Which engineers' reports come to you is set by the super admin in the approval matrix, separately for each project. Your approval makes the report final and emails a summary to the project's stakeholders.

Every evening

  1. Daily reports → Reviewed. These have been checked by your site engineers.
  2. Open each report. A clock runs while it is open; the time you take is recorded.
  3. Approve, or return or reject with a comment the site team will see.

Weekly

  • Performance: foremen, site engineers and reporting compliance for your projects.
  • Reports → Report handling time: who is slow to prepare, review or approve.
  • Presentation: build the site progress deck for your meeting.
What you do not seeCost and billing figures. Your executive summary and the reports you may open are set by the super admin in Settings → Role access.

Project managerApproves the record, then acts on it

Every evening, plus Monday review

Your approval turns site data into the company's record. Nothing reaches a dashboard, an invoice or a client report without it.

Every evening

  1. Open Daily reports, filter to Submitted. The hint line tells you how many are waiting.
  2. Open each report. You see the quantity, every crew and machine, the attachments, the remarks, and the full audit trail.
  3. Check three things: is the quantity credible for that crew, is the crew what was actually on site, and does the productivity index look explainable.
  4. Mark reviewed, then approve — or return with comments if something is wrong. Rejection and return both require a comment, which the site team sees immediately.

Every Monday

  1. Dashboard: productivity index for the last 7 days, workforce trend, early warnings.
  2. Executive summary → Project view: SPI, CPI, EAC, forecast finish, milestones, EOT status, bottlenecks.
  3. Reports → Early warnings: work down the list. Each warning names the indicator, the threshold and a recommended action.
  4. Reports → Manpower histogram: what has to be mobilised, by trade, for the next six weeks.
  5. Reports → EAC: where the project lands on cost, and what the variance at completion is.

Before the monthly meeting

  • Quantity reconciliation — how much was done by your own crews, by rental manpower and by subcontractors.
  • Unit rate analysis — which activities are costing more per unit than budgeted, and the revised rate for the rest of the work.
  • Invoiceable value — what can actually be billed this month after phase caps and retention.
  • Export the Full report pack to Excel, or individual reports to PDF.
Approve the same dayApproving late is the most common cause of a wrong dashboard. Submitted reports do count, but only approval closes the audit trail and protects you in a claim.

Planning engineerOwns the plan, the price weighting and the design and procurement update

At award, at every revision, and every week

At award, once per project

  1. Baselines → choose the project → Original baseline, then load the P6 file (XER, XML or the Excel template).
  2. Set the mapping in the preview: which WBS level is the work location, which level decides the activity class, and which resource carries the price weighting. The preview shows how many labour resources and hours it read, how many activities carry quantities, and which weighting resources exist.
  3. Read the warnings. If it says none of the activity IDs match the project, you have the wrong file. Check before importing; you can undo afterwards.
  4. Import, then check the baseline summary: activities per class, budget manhours, earliest start and latest finish.
  5. Map the P6 labour resources to trades in the panel underneath. Suggestions are pre-filled; one button creates any trades that don't exist yet, such as Lineman or Cable Jointer.
  6. Tell the costing engineer the baseline is loaded so they can download the costing template.

Every week

  1. Weekly update → Download update template. It contains only the design and procurement activities in your projects.
  2. Fill the shaded columns: data date, actual start, milestone reached, forecast finish, actual finish, remarks. Progress is set by milestone, never typed as a percentage.
  3. Upload it. Every row is validated: unknown IDs, construction rows, invalid milestones, dates after the data date, and progress going backwards without a remark are all rejected.
  4. Review the preview, then apply. The batch is stored with its data date and your name.
  5. Export the P6 progress update from Reports and load it into the schedule, so P6 and ProTrack tell the same story.

When the schedule changes

  • An approved extension of time → load a Revised baseline. A catch-up plan → load a Recovery baseline. Both store dates only and never change earned progress.
  • Use Measure against at the top of the screen to report on either, and the Baseline comparison report to show slip against each.
  • Re-importing the original baseline for a schedule already progressed in P6? Tick Ignore percent complete in the file, or the progress will be counted twice.
Keep milestones currentSettings → Projects → Edit holds project start, intermediate milestones, TCC, PAC, FAC and the EOT claim. The invoicing engine releases phase balances and retention against these dates, so they are commercial, not decorative.

Costing engineerOwns the budget: quantity, unit, rate, cost and manhours

At award, and at every budget revision

You keep quantities out of P6 and in a file you control. ProTrack joins the two on the activity ID.

At award

  1. Baselines → the project → Master Manpower Costing Sheet → Download the sheet template. It arrives pre-filled with every activity ID and name from the schedule.
  2. Fill in per activity: budget quantity, UOM, budget unit rate, budget cost, budget manhours, and a gang code if you use one. Leave budget cost blank and ProTrack computes quantity × rate.
  3. Several BOQ lines against one activity? Use several rows with the same activity ID; they are summed.
  4. Upload it. The preview shows how many rows matched the baseline, which activity IDs are not in the schedule, and how many schedule activities have no costing row.
  5. Check the flags: any activity whose manhours differ from the P6 loading by more than 25% is highlighted.
  6. Apply. Quantities, units, rates, costs and manhours are written to the activities, and the previous state is snapshotted for undo.

Purchase orders, for your projects

  1. Baselines → your project → Subcontractor purchase orders.
  2. Add a purchase order: pick the company, or New subcontractor; PO number, basis, date, value.
  3. Open it and add rates: per activity and unit for measured POs, per trade per manhour for manpower supply, each with an effective date.
  4. Watch cost to date against the PO value in the same table.

Trade salaries stay with the super admin, in Settings → Trades and rates.

Every month

  • Unit rate analysis — budgeted rate against actual, split into own labour, rental manpower and subcontract, with a forecast cost to complete at the actual rate.
  • Quantity reconciliation — scope against executed, by execution route.
  • Budget manhour reconciliation — where your file and the schedule disagree, worst first. Each project names which is the budget of record.
  • EAC — the forecast final cost, and the variance at completion.
Price is not yoursThe client price schedule sits in P6 as the weighting resource, loaded by planning, and drives invoicing. Your file drives cost and productivity. Keeping them apart is what lets ProTrack show margin: earned price less incurred cost.

Executive, regional and portfolio managersDecide where to intervene, with no data entry

Weekly, and before every review meeting

Executives see every project; regional managers see their regions; portfolio managers see one region. All read-only.

The five-minute weekly scan

  1. Executive summary. Portfolio complete against plan, SPI, CPI, productivity, and how many projects need attention.
  2. Projects by region and by sector. Tap any row to filter the whole system to it.
  3. Critical projects. Worst first, with Plan %, Actual %, variance, CPI, SPI, productivity and the main issue in plain words.
  4. Open the worst project in Project view: progress, forecast finish, EAC, milestones with TCC and EOT status, bottlenecks, underperforming activities and foreman performance.
  5. Ask RASA anything the screen doesn't answer: "what is the invoiceable value", "which subcontractor is underperforming", "how many more riggers do we need".

The four questions worth asking in the meeting

Is progress real?Actual % against plan, and the productivity index behind it. Progress without productivity means more people, not better performance.
Will it finish on time?Forecast finish and slip, with the reason in the bottlenecks list.
Will it make money?CPI, EAC and variance at completion, plus the unit rate analysis for where the money is going.
Can we bill it?Invoiceable value: phase caps, milestone releases and retention, which is usually well below the progress percentage.
One number to trustThe productivity index. It cannot be improved by adding people, unlike percent complete, and it turns before the schedule does.

Super admin, the planning manager at head officeOwns the system, the master data and who can see what

At setup, then as things change

Setting up, in order

  1. Sectors, regions and cities. Assign a regional manager to each region.
  2. Trades and rates. Create every labour category and set its cost rate from basic salary, hours per month and the loaded factor. Rates are dated.
  3. Projects. Code (any format, such as PE-000 or EL-000), name, sector, region, city, dates, contract value, project type and status.
  4. Users and access. Create each person with their role, and tick the projects or regions they may see.
  5. Project team. Reopen the project and set the project manager, site engineer and foremen.
  6. Milestones and EOT for the project, including TCC, PAC and FAC.
  7. Payment terms: phase caps, milestone releases, retention percentage and cap, the TCC and FAC release split, advance payment and recovery.
  8. Budget manhours source: Master Manpower Costing Sheet or P6 resource loading, per project.
  9. Settings → Approval matrix: on every project, assign each foreman a reviewing site engineer and each site engineer an approving site manager. A person on two projects is assigned on each. Aim for no project heading showing "not assigned".
  10. Settings → Email: choose which roles receive the approval summary.
  11. Hand subcontractor purchase orders to each project's costing engineer. You can still review every PO in Settings → Subcontractors.
  12. Hand over to planning and costing to load the baseline and the Master Manpower Costing Sheet.

Routine administration

  • New starter: Users and access → Create user → role → tick projects. Give them the demo password and tell them to use Forgot password.
  • Leaver: set the user Inactive. Their history stays intact.
  • Salary revision: Trades and rates → the trade → Add a new rate with its effective date. Past reports keep their old rate.
  • New purchase order: done by the project's costing engineer on the Baselines page; you can review or correct it in Settings → Subcontractors.
  • Wrong baseline loaded: Baselines → the project → Undo this import, or Remove imported baseline.
  • Check the change log in Settings. Every change carries a name and a time stamp.
Access disciplineA portfolio manager covers exactly one region; a regional manager can cover several. Approvals stay with project managers. Only planning engineers and you load baselines or the weekly update. Keep it that way, or the audit trail loses meaning.
