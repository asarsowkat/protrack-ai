ProTrack user guide

Construction productivity intelligence: from the daily site report to management decisions, invoices and cash.

ProTrack turns what the site records every evening into productivity, earned value, forecast cost, invoiceable value and early warnings, and it measures the people who feed it. Its principle is one sentence: quantity alone is progress; quantity together with the resources that produced it is productivity. Everything follows from capturing both, once, at site.

For site teamsA daily report that takes under a minute per activity, on a phone, with the productivity index shown as you type.
For planning and costingP6 in, P6 out; a Master Manpower Costing Sheet for budget quantity and rate; a weekly updated P6 schedule for progress, including design and procurement; an invoice register.
For managementThe Executive report (released), customisable per person, and the Live summary of working data: progress, SPI, CPI, EAC, invoiceable against invoiced, approved and collected, and who is late.

2. How the application works

ProTrack is a single chain. Each link is owned by one role, and every later link depends on the ones before it.

  1. Set up. The super admin creates the project, users and access, trades and salary rates, milestones including TCC, PAC and FAC, and the contract payment terms.
  2. Baseline. The planning engineer imports the P6 schedule. ProTrack reads activities, WBS, dates, labour hours, material quantities, the price weighting resource and the trade mix, then runs the DCMA 14-point check.
  3. Budget and purchase orders. The costing engineer downloads the costing template, pre-filled with every activity ID, adds budget quantity, unit, unit rate, cost and manhours, and uploads it. He then enters each subcontractor's purchase order for the project, with its rates.
  4. Daily reporting. Every working day the foreman or site engineer reports, per activity, the quantity executed and the crews that produced it: own labour, subcontractors with their trades, and equipment.
  5. Review and approval. The site engineer reviews what the foreman submitted; the site manager approves it the same evening. The project manager can do either. Nothing counts until it is submitted, and only approval closes the audit trail. Every step is timed.
  6. Weekly updated schedule. The planning engineer uploads the updated P6 XER each week. It carries progress for every activity, including design and procurement; there is no separate Excel update.
  7. Invoicing. The costing engineer uploads the invoice register weekly: invoice number, date, type, phase (progress invoices), amount, and the submitted, approved and collected amounts with their dates, including the advance.
  8. Analysis. ProTrack calculates productivity, earned value, EAC, manpower forecasts, unit rates, reconciliation and invoiceable value against the contract terms, and raises early warnings.
  9. Oversight. Management reads the executive summary, the reports and RASA; the performance scorecards show who is keeping the chain alive, and reminders chase the gaps.
Why the order mattersCosting matches on activity IDs, so it cannot come before the baseline. Productivity needs the planned rate, so it cannot come before costing or the P6 labour loading. Invoiceable value needs the price weighting and the payment terms. If a screen reads zero, the link before it is usually missing.

Approval matrix

The super admin decides, project by project, who reviews and who approves. In Settings → Approval matrix each project lists its foremen and site engineers, with a choice beside each name:

  • Foreman → site engineer who reviews his reports on that project
  • Site engineer → site manager who approves the reports he has reviewed on that project

Only people with access to that project are offered. Someone working on several projects is assigned separately on each, so the chain follows the site team of each project. Anyone left unassigned has that step done by the project manager, and the project heading says how many are unassigned.

Approval email

The moment a report is approved, ProTrack emails a summary to everyone with access to that project in the chosen roles: by default site engineers, site managers, project managers, portfolio managers, planning and costing engineers, executives and super admins. Site engineers and site managers receive only the reports they reviewed or approved. Recipients are blind-copied.

The email carries the project, date, discipline and foreman; activities, manpower, manhours and productivity; each activity's quantity today, percent complete and index; anything below 85% productivity; site remarks; who prepared, reviewed and approved it and how long each took; and an Open <report number> in ProTrack button that goes straight to the report after sign-in.

The super admin chooses the roles, or switches it off, in Settings → Email, which also lists every email with its recipients, status and a preview. Sending needs the mail function set up on the server once; until then emails are prepared and listed but not sent. The menu item is Administration → Approval email; it opens Settings → Email. Sent emails lists the daily report emails, the report emails and the reporting status reminders, with recipients, status and a preview.

Report emails (v3.17). When an internal report (monthly, and weekly unless switched off) or a monthly cost report is approved and released, ProTrack emails the released figures with a link that opens the report:

  • an internal report: planned, actual, SPI, forecast finish, the baseline comparison and the narrative;
  • a cost report: budget, actual, EAC, VAC, commitments, the internal contract value and the gross margin.

The super admin sets each list in Settings → Email → Report emails:

  • roles: everyone in the role with access to the project. The defaults are project managers, portfolio managers, regional managers, executives, and planning engineers for internal reports or costing engineers for cost reports.
  • named people, on any project;
  • other addresses.

A section planning manager sets the internal report list for their region in Planning & Scheduling → Internal Reporting (Report email for your region); a region without its own list uses the default.

Release strategy

Administration → Release strategy (super admin). ProTrackAI goes live stage by stage.

  1. Stages. One to six stages, each with a name (up to 40 characters) and a planned go-live date. A later stage cannot go live before an earlier one. Add a stage; Remove the last stage once no module is in it.
  2. Modules. Put each of the eleven modules in a stage: Daily Reports, Planning & Scheduling, Projects & Handover, Project document rack, Executive report & reporting status, Cost Control, Materials & Procurement, Claims & Variations, Invoices & Cash Flow, BOQ Engine, Reports & Dashboards. The proposed plan: stage 1 Daily Reports, Planning, Projects, Reports; stage 2 Executive report and reporting status, Document rack, Cost Control; stage 3 Materials, Claims, Invoices; stage 4 BOQ Engine.
  3. Live up to. Choose the stage, or Nothing yet (before go-live). Add a comment if useful.
  4. Hide the modules not live yet from the menu (optional). Super admins still see them. RASA, Imports & Integrations and Administration are never hidden.
  5. Save the release strategy, or Discard the changes. Each save is listed under History with who, when, the stages and the comment.

RASA answers only from live modules; about one not live yet it names its stage. A super admin can still ask, and the answer is marked Test answer. Until a plan is saved every module is live. Document text for RASA shows how many documents have their text read; Read their text now reads the rest. On the server this needs the v34 update (STAGE-28-SETUP); until then every module counts as live.

Internal weightage and the updated schedule

Two weightages apply to every project. The client weightage is the weighting in the P6 schedule agreed with the client; it drives invoiceable value and the client report. The internal weightage is the % of eight phases agreed with management (general works, design, material, construction, installation, testing & commissioning, energization, project close out); it drives every ProTrackAI progress figure and the internal report.

  1. Once per project: Planning & Scheduling → Baselines and P6 import → Internal weightage by phase: enter the % of each phase (total 100%, shown as you type) and Save weightage. Energization is a phase of its own from v3.23; on older projects it has no weight until you give it one. Once an updated schedule has been loaded, a change needs a reason. Every change is kept in the history.
  2. Each week or month: in Updated schedule, tick what the schedule is loaded with (Client weightage, normally, or Internal weightage) and Choose updated XER (XER only, up to 40 MB).
  3. Check the preview: the data date (enter it if the file has none), Client weighting taken from (a weighting resource, planned labour units or planned duration), Phase taken from (the main WBS branch, or a P6 activity code such as "Phase"), and the phase of each branch or code value. ProTrackAI suggests a phase from the name. With internal weightage set, every weighted activity of a client-weighted schedule must have a phase before you can save.
  4. Read the results, then Save updated schedule: client planned and actual, internal planned and actual, TCC, PAC and FAC, and planned and actual by phase. A phase with weightage but no activity in the schedule is left out and the others are scaled up; the preview says which.
  5. ProTrackAI works out, at the data date: phase % = Σ(weight × %) ÷ Σ weight within the phase; client % = the same over all activities; internal % = Σ(phase weightage × phase %) ÷ Σ phase weightage of the phases in the schedule. Planned % follows the baseline dates (ProTrackAI's where the activity is in the baseline).
  6. Every progress figure (project overview, dashboard, executive summary, reports, RASA, internal report) then uses the internal %. Invoiceable value uses the client price weighting × the schedule's % complete. Daily reports still drive productivity and manpower, and construction progress until a first updated schedule is loaded.
  • Same file twice: nothing changes.
  • Late alert: saving warns, in the notifications, when a design or procurement item is forecast more than 7 days after its baseline finish.
  • Its own baseline (v3.14): an XER exported with its baseline keeps that baseline's dates apart, as the baseline assigned in the updated schedule; only the schedule's own activities are read.
  • Material tracking: a project without Material tracking is offered its set-up from the schedule (v3.8.1).
  • Updates loaded lists every update with its data date, file, weightage, client and internal plan / actual, milestones and who loaded it. Updates cannot be changed or deleted. The activity rows are kept for the latest updates of each project, which is what the reports' baseline comparison, phase table and E&P tab read.
  • The updated schedule is the source of engineering and procurement progress too: there is no separate E&P upload (v3.8.4).

Cash-out forecast

Invoices & Cash Flow → Cash-out forecast forecasts each project's cash-out against its approved current budget, the control limit.

The screen

Choose the project at the top; the line under it gives the reporting cutoff, the currency (SAR), the approved budget used and the forecast version. Six tabs:

  • Overview: the status (an overrun shows a red alert with the amount, the % and whether the management review is recorded); eight cards: approved current budget, actual cumulative cash-out, forecast remaining, forecast at completion, budget headroom or overrun, forecast budget utilization, outstanding PO payment obligations, data reconciliation status; the charts, the monthly table and the data exceptions.
  • PO payment schedules: material POs first, then subcontract POs (Open one to keep its terms), the cost report heads and the other cash-out lines; materials not ordered yet are listed with their cost in the ETC.
  • Finance actuals: the import, the opening balance, every cumulative balance with its movement, and the upload and correction history.
  • Reconciliation, Versions and scenarios, Portfolio (every project in your access, with totals).
  • Approved current budget: the Approved budget (Rev-0 + approved supplements) of the latest released monthly cost report. Without one, the project is Budget Not Configured; PO values are never used as a budget.
  • Actual: Finance's cumulative cash-out per month. The month's actual is this cumulative minus the previous one; the first balance is an opening balance unless a zero opening is verified.
  • Forecast remaining: the unpaid net amount of each PO payment milestone at its expected payment date, plus the remaining amount of the other cash-out lines (budgeted cash-out no PO covers).
  • Forecast at completion = actual cumulative + forecast remaining. Headroom = budget − forecast at completion. Utilization = forecast at completion ÷ budget.
  • Status, in this order: Budget Not Configured, Actual Overrun, Forecast Overrun, Forecast Incomplete, Reconciliation Required, Within Budget.
  1. Import Finance actuals (super admin, Head Office cost control coordinator): choose the projects and the reporting cutoff, then the file. The template, or Finance's own layout with its columns mapped once. Review the preview (new, restatement, already imported, refused). A restatement needs the correction tick and Finance's reason; the original is kept.
  2. Keep the PO payment terms (costing engineers): open a PO, add its milestones (% of the PO value or a fixed amount, event, dates, contractual payment days, retention, status, evidence) or use the standard 10 / 20 / 20 / 40 / 10. They must total 100%. Mark a milestone paid only with its Finance evidence. The coordinator, the Head or a super admin approves the terms; the first approved payment date is the baseline.
  3. Record the other cash-out lines: what Finance has paid on them to date and what remains, over which months.
  4. Reconcile: the actual against the plan and the forecast; the unallocated actual cash-out (Finance's total less the paid milestones and lines) shows what still needs reconciling. Finance's project-level figures are never shared out among POs.
  5. Publish: save the live forecast as a version; someone else reviews it; the Head or a super admin publishes it. A forecast over budget is published only after the management review records the decision and the action. Scenarios (optimistic, pessimistic, your own) are simulations that change nothing.
  6. Report: Excel or PDF for a project, Excel for the portfolio, each stating the cutoff, currency, budget revision, forecast version and time.

Material POs and the cost report (v3.21). The cash-out forecast is built from three sources:

  • Material POs: every PO number of Material tracking has its value and payment terms (Material tracking → Payment terms under the PO number). Each milestone is paid at a Material tracking stage (PO creation, FAT, delivery…), the P6 TCC or final acceptance, or a fixed date, plus the contractual payment days. The stage date is the actual once done, else the P6 forecast of the latest updated schedule. The standard schedule is 10% PO, 20% FAT, 60% delivery and 10% retention at TCC.
  • Subcontract POs: as before.
  • The monthly cost report, by WBS head (Cost report heads). Material heads go to the material POs, installation heads to the subcontract POs, and contingency, management reserve, prime cost and provisional sums are excluded. Every other head forecasts its EAC − actual, spread evenly to the P6 forecast TCC. A PO head also forecasts its ETC (not yet ordered).

In Reconciliation, Finance's cumulative less the paid milestones and the cost booked gives the difference. Cost booked but not yet paid is a payable in the next month. Milestones due but not marked paid and retention are timing items. Only what remains is unexplained. The cash forecast at completion is cross-checked with the cost report EAC.

Rules worth knowing

  • Tolerance: unexplained cash-out above 1% of the actual, and at least SAR 100,000, makes the status Reconciliation Required.
  • Cross-check: a gap of more than 5% between the cash forecast at completion and the cost report EAC without the excluded heads is flagged: the PO values or the head treatments do not match the cost report.
  • Head treatments: the keepers can change the treatment of any head of the latest cost report and switch off the ETC of the PO heads (Save the head treatments). A head belongs in one place only. A PO head with no live SAR PO falls back to the cost report, with a warning; a cancelled or non-SAR PO does not cover a head (v3.26.1).
  • Other cash-out lines need the confirmation that neither a PO nor a cost report head covers them, and the Finance reference for what is paid to date.
  • PO changes: Record the PO revision (approved revised value, reference, date, reason) keeps the original value. Cancelled needs a reason; its unpaid milestones leave the forecast. A paid milestone cannot be removed; only the coordinator, the Head or a super admin changes it, with a comment.
  • Finance import: each import is numbered (FCO-0001 …) and listed in the Import centre; a file with nothing new is refused; rows are classified again when you confirm.
  • Versions: Save the live forecast as a draft version → reviewed by the costing coordinator, the Head or a super admin (not its author) → Publish by the Head or a super admin. A forecast over budget is published only after the management review: the Head or a super admin chooses the decision (corrective action agreed; budget revision to be requested; accepted for monitoring; data to be corrected) and describes the action. A published version is kept.

Rules BOQ Engine keeps:

  • Nothing is guessed. Values read from a PDF are proposals for a person to confirm. A missing input makes a quantity Incomplete, never zero. Rates are never invented. Every figure is labelled an estimate.
  • Someone else checks. The person who registered a drawing or set its status does not confirm it. The author of a calculation, or the last person to change it, does not verify it. The author of an assumption does not review or approve it. A super admin is exempt from these three, but not from the next one.
  • Nobody reviews, approves or releases an estimate revision they prepared or submitted, super admins included.
  • Released never changes. Approved and released lines are frozen; each revision keeps its own copy of its calculations.
  • Budgets are protected. BOQ Engine writes only to its own records. A hand-off to cost control, procurement or cash flow is a recorded request only.
  • Every action is in the audit history (Settings & Audit History).

Project & WBS Setup and the completeness check

The order is: project (from the ProTrackAI project master, not copied) → estimation package → structure → drawings → dimensions → calculations → Estimated BOQ.

  1. New package. Give the package code (unique on the project), name, contract or package if any, site or location, and the substation voltage or project type. Notes are optional.
  2. Add a structure. Choose the facility, discipline (Civil, Structural, Architectural, Electrical & Underground) and structure or system type; give it a name unique in the package (for example Transformer T1 Foundation); pick the WBS code from the P6 baseline if there is one; add a location if useful. The same type can appear many times, each with its own name.
  3. Completeness. Open a structure's completeness: each required drawing or specification is Available, Missing, Not Applicable, Awaiting Clarification or Available but Unverified (for example a scanned sheet that cannot be read). An estimator marks a requirement Not applicable or Awaiting clarification, always with a reason, or clears the mark.
  4. Read the result. The structure is Ready, Partial estimate only (quantities that do not depend on the missing items can be calculated; they are provisional in the Estimated BOQ) or Not ready (register the drawings first).

Drawings: upload, register, review and measure

Drawing Upload & Classification (upload right):

  1. Choose the PDFs and press Upload and read. PDF only (export DWG or DXF to PDF first), up to 40 MB each by default. Refused: a file that is not a real PDF, a password-protected PDF, a PDF with scripts, launch actions or embedded files, and the same file already uploaded on the project.
  2. Wait for the reading. The original is kept unchanged and read in the background from its text layer: drawing number, revision, scale, title, units, a proposed structure type and discipline with how sure it is, the drawing contents and the dimension texts with their page and position. Processing shows each job with its progress, attempts and error; Retry or Read again is safe. Closing the tab stops a job; it shows as failed and can be retried.
  3. Register. In Waiting for registration, press Register and check every proposed value against the title block. Mandatory: package, site, facility, voltage or project type, discipline, drawing category, structure type, the specific structures, title, number, revision, issue date (not in the future), drawing status, scale (1:50, NTS, As noted or Dimensions only) and units (mm or m). The same number and revision cannot be registered twice. Discard removes an upload not yet registered.

Drawing statuses. Approved, Approved with Comments, Issued for Construction (IFC) and As-Built count for estimating, but only after a reviewer who did not register the drawing confirms them. Preliminary, For Information, Design Development, Issued for Review, Issued for Approval, Superseded and Rejected do not count; a quantity using such a drawing is provisional.

Drawing Register. One row per drawing number, latest revision first and older revisions below; filters for search, discipline, structure type, drawing status and Needing verification only. Details opens the drawing full screen:

  • Confirm the … status (review right, not the person who registered it or set the status).
  • Change status (upload right, with a reason; calculations using it are marked Recalculation Required) and Edit details.
  • Make this revision the estimation basis (estimate or review right, with a confirmation). The first registered revision of a number is the basis automatically; a later one becomes the basis only when someone confirms it.
  • Mark superseded (review right; say what supersedes it). A revision is never overwritten or deleted.
  • Upload a new revision, Review and measure, Download the original. The revisions and the history are listed below.

Drawing Review & Measurement (upload right to change anything):

  1. Open the drawing. Choose it; move between pages; zoom Fit, 2× or 4×. Extracted dimensions are boxed on the page. A scanned page says that nothing could be read from it.
  2. Check each dimension you will use. Verify, Correct (a changed value needs a reason, and the quantities that use it are recalculated) or Reject (with a reason). Give it a name, for example foundation length L, so calculations and later revisions can find it. A confidence is how sure the reading is, never an approval; only verified values feed verified quantities.
  3. Calibrate before measuring. Choose Calibrate, click the two ends of a dimension you can read, and give its real length with its unit (for example 6000 mm). It is checked against the written scale; a difference over 2% is flagged.
  4. Measure. Choose Measure and click two points. The distance is added as an unverified dimension, to verify like any other. Add a dimension by hand for anything else.

Quantity Calculation Register

One row per calculation: code, structure, template, quantities, status, source drawings and assumptions, who verified it. Filter by structure, status, or search.

  1. New calculation (estimate right). Choose the structure, the template and, where asked, the item category, and write a description. The formula is shown.
  2. Give every input a source: a dimension from a drawing of that structure, a specification requirement, an approved assumption, the volume of another calculation, or Entered by a person with a note saying where it comes from. Add zones, segments or faces as the template needs.
  3. Wastage or allowance is added only with a documented basis (a specification requirement or an approved assumption). Nothing else is added by default: no working space, side slope, bulking or compaction factor unless it is an input with its own source.
  4. Work it out, then Save. The result shows net, allowance and estimated quantity with the working, the engine version and the date. Changing a saved calculation asks for the reason: changed geometry, specification or assumption, corrected extraction, changed measurement rule, added or deleted scope, or manual adjustment.
  5. Verify. An estimator or reviewer who did not prepare it or last change it presses Verify, once every input is verified.

Statuses: Needs Verification, Verified, Incomplete (an input is missing; no quantity is given), Calculation Error, Recalculation Required (a drawing, dimension, specification or assumption behind it changed; Recalculate first). Remove needs a reason; a calculation in an approved or released revision stays.

Templates: excavation in rectangular zones; strip or trench excavation by chainage; cable trench by chainage (direct buried); backfilling and disposal; concrete (segments less openings); formwork by face; reinforcement from a bar schedule; reinforcement from diameter and spacing; measured items by volume, area, length or number. Quantities are worked out in exact decimals and kept unrounded; rounding is for display only.

Specifications & Assumptions

  • Register a document (upload or estimate right): kind (technical specification, approved construction standard, project-specific notes, measurement rules), reference, revision, title, and whether it applies to the whole project or one package. A PDF is optional; its text is read for proposed requirements (concrete and steel grades, compaction, wastage, bedding, cover).
  • Requirements are proposals until an estimator or reviewer Verifies them; Reject or Change as needed. Add a requirement by hand with its clause; someone else verifies a requirement you entered, unless you have the review right.
  • Conflicts: two values for the same topic that apply to the same thing are shown in a warning. Reject the one that does not apply; none is chosen automatically.
  • Record an assumption (estimate right): assumption or exclusion, description, reason, source, value and unit if any, affected items. It is reviewed (review right) and then approved or rejected (approve right), each by someone other than its author. A reviewer can return it, saying what to correct; a rejection needs a reason. Changing an approved assumption asks why; Withdraw needs a reason. The calculations that use it are recalculated when it is approved, rejected or withdrawn.

Estimated BOQ, review and release, revisions and export

Estimated BOQ. Choose Live calculations or a revision, a view (by discipline, by structure, by trench type, location totals or package totals) and search. Each line has its item, description, structure and WBS, quantity, unit, status (provisional if the drawing package is incomplete or a drawing is not eligible), source calculation and drawings, rate and amount. A line without a result has no quantity, never a zero. Click the item for its detail:

  • Drawing-Based Estimated Quantity, or Internally Released Estimated Quantity for a released revision;
  • Approved BOQ, approved budget and actual quantities, each shown as Not available and never merged;
  • the calculation, formula, drawings and pages, specifications, assumptions and every input, with show on the drawing.

Rates are optional: a person with the rate right types them on a revision being prepared, with where each comes from (rate library, quotation, cost report). Amount = quantity × rate. Without rates it is a quantity-only BOQ. A new revision starts with the rates of the last approved or released one.

Review & Approval Workflow, per package:

  1. Start a new revision (estimate right). Only one revision of a package is prepared at a time; revisions are numbered from 0.
  2. Generate the draft Estimated BOQ. It takes the current calculations and keeps a copy of them with the revision.
  3. Submit for review. Refused if a calculation changed after the draft was generated (generate it again) or if a line has a calculation error or needs recalculation.
  4. Start the review (review right, not the preparer or submitter). Either reviewer or approver can Return for correction, saying what to correct.
  5. Approve for internal use (approve right, not the preparer or submitter). Every line with a quantity must be verified, and every line must still match its calculation.
  6. Release (approve right, not the preparer or submitter). The previous released revision becomes Superseded.
  7. Hand off to cost control… (approve right, released revisions only). Choose the destination (budget by WBS head, budget supplement request, purchase requisition, or cash-out forecast), describe the mapping, and tick the confirmation. It is recorded as Awaiting cost-control interface; nothing is written to that module.

Statuses: Draft → In Progress → Ready for Review → Under Review → Approved for Internal Use → Released → Superseded, or Returned for Correction. Each revision shows who prepared, submitted, reviewed, approved and released it, with its history.

Revision Comparison.

  • New drawing revisions: each registered revision that is not yet the basis, the calculations that use the old revision, and its named or used dimensions against the old ones. Make rev … the estimation basis (estimate or review right) marks those calculations Recalculation Required.
  • Calculations needing recalculation: Re-link to the new basis moves inputs to the matching verified dimensions of the new revision (by name, or same page and text), or Recalculate as is (estimate right).
  • Quantity changes between estimate revisions: choose the previous and the new revision (or live calculations). Each item is Unchanged, Added, Removed or Modified, with the variance (new − previous), the % only when the previous quantity is not zero, and the source of the difference from the calculations' recorded change reasons.

6. Step by step, by role

What each role does and when, in the order of a working week. The screens and rights behind each step are in Screens each role sees and Rights each role has. A head office position (Head of Planning and Cost Control, costing coordinator, planning section manager) adds its own steps to the person's role.

Foreman — every working day, about five minutes

  1. Sign in on your phone. ProTrackAI opens on Daily reports. You see daily reports, new report, actions and approvals and your own performance, for your projects only.
  2. Look at Actions and approvals first. A report returned to you is listed there (returned, fix and resubmit); the comment is in the report. Open it, fix it and submit it again the same day.
  3. New report. Date and project are filled in; choose yourself as foreman.
  4. Work location, work type, WBS, activity. Each choice narrows the next, so the activity list is short.
  5. Read the activity's figures: total quantity (scope), executed up to yesterday, balance, balance duration (working days to the planned finish) and plan for today = balance ÷ balance duration. Before the planned start the plan is 0; after the planned finish the whole balance is due.
  6. Actual quantity today — the total placed on that activity, including anything done by subcontractors. The form shows the total to date with today. If it goes beyond the scope, an amber Beyond the scope box appears: check the figure; if it is right, submit it. It is accepted, and your site engineer justifies it.
  7. Own labour — trade, workers, regular and overtime hours. The usual crew is pre-filled; correct what differs.
  8. Subcontractor — choose the company once, enter the quantity they executed, then Add trade for each of their trades with workers and hours. A second company is a second block.
  9. Equipment — type, units, hours.
  10. Add another activity for everything else your crews did today.
  11. Check the productivity panel. It shows the combined index and, where a subcontractor worked, your own figure and theirs separately. An amber line means a subcontractor quantity has no hours.
  12. Remarks and photos — delays, access, weather, instructions.
  13. Submit. It goes to your site engineer for review, then your site manager for approval. If you are interrupted, press Save draft and open it later from Daily reports → Draft.
  14. Monthly: Reports & Dashboards → Performance → Foremen, your own row: productivity, overtime, rework, approval rate and your score.
  15. Any day: ask RASA for Critical activities, Manpower or a Progress report; it also lists your drafts not yet submitted.

A clock at the top of the form starts when you open it and stops when you save or submit; it carries on if you return to a saved draft. Time with the app in the background is not counted.

Site engineer — review every foreman's report, daily

  1. Every morning: Home → Live summary (working data), your home page: progress, SPI, productivity and the projects needing attention.
  2. Actions and approvals → Mine lists the foremen's reports waiting for your review; a report is late after a day. Open each from the list, or from Daily reports → Submitted. A timer runs while it is open.
  3. Quantities beyond the scope are marked "Beyond scope" in the list. You cannot mark the report reviewed until each one is justified.   4. Mark as reviewed once quantity and crews are right, or Return with comments. It then goes to the site manager assigned to you on that project. You only see the Review button on reports of the foremen assigned to you.
  5. Daily reports → Draft. Nothing should stay in draft overnight. When a foreman cannot submit, open his draft and press Submit for approval yourself.
  6. Check quantities against survey or measurement, and crews against the gate count. Until the site manager approves you can still Edit report; your edits are logged, and a quantity beyond the scope changed after your review must be justified again.
  7. Deal with returned reports the same day; the manager's comment is in the audit trail.
  8. Weekly: Live summary → Project view for bottlenecks, underperforming activities and foreman performance; Reports & Dashboards → All reports for Planned vs actual productivity, the Manpower histogram and Early warnings.
  9. When you need a drawing or a schedule: Projects & Handover → Project document rack, Planning & Scheduling row.
  10. Monthly: Performance → Site engineers (your own row) and Foremen of your projects.

Site manager — approve every reviewed report, the same day

  1. Every morning: Home → Live summary (working data), your home page.

7. How performance is measured

Every scorecard is calculated from data the person already enters. No score depends on anyone's opinion. Unless stated, windows are the last 30 days of submitted or approved reports.

Foreman score, out of 100

Score = min(PI, 1.2) ÷ 1.2 × 60 + approval rate × 25 + (1 − rework rate) × 15
  • PI — productivity index of his reports: earned manhours ÷ actual manhours, capped at 1.2 so a single freak day cannot dominate.
  • Approval rate — approved ÷ (approved + rejected).
  • Rework rate — reports returned or rejected ÷ reports submitted.

Site engineer score, out of 100

Score = compliance × 30 + timeliness × 25 + approval rate × 20 + min(PI, 1.2) ÷ 1.2 × 25
  • Compliance — working days with a report ÷ working days in the window.
  • Timeliness — reports not flagged late ÷ reports.
  • Approval rate and PI as for foremen, for the whole project.

  • DCMA pass rate — checks passed ÷ checks assessable, averaged across his projects' imported XERs (section 8).
  • Updated schedule compliance — weeks with an updated P6 schedule ÷ weeks elapsed, over the last twelve weeks (or since the baseline starts). E&P Excel updates loaded before version 3.8.4 count for the weeks they cover.
  • Where either part cannot be measured yet, it counts as 50% so a new project neither rewards nor punishes.

Costing engineer score, out of 100

Score = upload quality × 40 + costing coverage × 20 + timeliness × 40
  • Upload quality — 1 − (rejected rows + ½ × flagged rows) ÷ rows uploaded, across all costing uploads.
  • Costing coverage — activities with a costing row ÷ activities in the baseline.
  • Timeliness — weeks in the last twelve with an invoice register upload ÷ weeks (fewer for a newer project).

Reporting compliance score, by project, out of 100

Score = 100 − 5 × missing report days − min(20, 3 × age of oldest pending report in days) − 10 if updated schedule overdue − 5 if invoice register overdue
  • Missing report days — working days in the last 14 (Fridays excluded) with no submitted report.
  • Updated schedule overdue — the latest updated P6 schedule is more than 9 days old, or none is loaded, where the project has a baseline.
  • Invoice register overdue — more than 9 days old, where payment terms are set.
  • Issues are graded: no report for more than a day is High; more than two missing days, an approval backlog, or an overdue updated schedule is Medium; an overdue register is Low.

Scores are coloured green at 85 and above, amber from 60, red below 60.

9. Formula reference

Productivity

Planned rate = budget quantity ÷ budget manhours
Earned manhours = quantity executed ÷ planned rate
Actual manhours = own workers × (regular + overtime hours) + subcontractor workers × hours
Productivity index (PI) = earned manhours ÷ actual manhours — green above 100%, amber 90–100%, red below 90%
Own quantity = quantity executed − subcontractor quantities; own and subcontract PI are shown separately

Earned value

Budget value per activity = costing budget cost, else P6 price weight, else manhours × blended rate
SPI = EV ÷ PV · CPI = EV ÷ AC · SV = EV − PV · CV = EV − AC
EAC = BAC ÷ CPI, or AC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI) · VAC = BAC − EAC · TCPI = (BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC)

Cost

Trade hourly rate = basic salary ÷ hours per month × loaded factor, taken from the rate effective on the report date
Own labour = workers × (regular + 1.5 × overtime) × trade rate
Measured subcontract = quantity × PO unit rate · Manpower supply = workers × hours × PO manhour rate

Contract value (v3.11)

Considered internally (per item) = client approved price once approved by the client, else the price considered internally with its WF number and date, else 0; 0 when rejected
Planned % against a baseline = Σ weight × elapsed share of that baseline's duration at the data date ÷ Σ weight (same activities and weightage as the headline) · Variance = actual − planned · SPI = actual ÷ planned
Revised contract value (internal) = original + Σ considered internally · Revised contract value (client) = original + Σ client approved price
Gross margin (internal) = revised contract value (internal) − EAC · Internal planned amount = revised contract value (internal) × planned %

Planning

Planned headcount = baseline daily quantity ÷ planned rate ÷ 8, split by the P6 trade mix
Forecast headcount = planned headcount × (planned rate ÷ actual rate over 21 days) · To mobilise = forecast − current

Unit rates and reconciliation

Actual cost per unit = executed cost ÷ quantity executed, split into own, rental and subcontract
Carried in + own + rental + subcontract = executed · Executed + remaining = scope

10. Billing and collection

Invoiceable value

Earned price per phase = phase price × price progress, where price comes from the P6 weighting resource
Billable per phase = min(earned price, phase price × progress cap) + releases whose milestone is achieved
Net invoiceable = Σ billable − separate retention (if any) + retention released − advance recovered

Example: construction 80% on progress, 10% at TCC, 10% at FAC. At full progress before TCC, 80% is billable. Set separate retention to zero unless the contract deducts it on top of the phase split. Advance recovery is a percentage of each invoice and stops once the advance is repaid. Blank or zero? A blank progress cap means 100%; 0 means nothing is billed on progress, only at the milestone releases. A blank retention cap means no cap; a retention percentage with a 0% cap is refused, because nothing would be held. Terms saved before version 2.4 keep their earlier meaning, where 0 was read as no cap.

Since v3.26.1 invoiceable is one figure everywhere (invoice register, executive report, cost report and the cash pages): on the client contract value, including the advance.

Payment terms per project (super admin)

Settings → Projects and terms → edit the project → Billing and payment terms:

  • Per phase (the payment-terms phases): the share billed on progress (blank = 100%, 0 = only at the releases), and up to two releases, each at a milestone (TCC, PAC, FAC, Start, Intermediate or a project milestone) with its %. Each row should total 100%; more than 100% is refused.
  • Separate retention on each invoice (%, 0 if none), its cap (% of contract price, blank for no cap), and the % released at TCC and at FAC (together at most 100%). A retention with a 0% cap is refused.
  • Advance payment (% of contract) and its recovery per invoice (%), until repaid.
  • Client pays within (days of submission): 0 to 365, default 60 (v3.15). The cash-inflow forecast collects each invoice after this period.

Press Save payment terms. Invoiceable value, the cash-inflow forecast, monthly invoicing and the reporting compliance check all use these terms.

Cash-inflow forecast

Invoices & Cash Flow → Cash-inflow forecast, by project. Invoices are raised at month end and collected after the client's payment period. The payment period is set in Settings → Projects and terms → Billing and payment terms, as Client pays within (days of submission); the default is 60 days.

  • New project (nothing invoiced): the whole inflow is worked out from the client baseline (original baseline dates) and the payment terms:
    • each phase is billed on progress up to its cap, plus its releases at the milestones;
    • retention and advance recovery are deducted from each invoice;
    • the retention is released at TCC and FAC;
    • the advance comes at the start.
    If the project has started with nothing invoiced, what the baseline earned so far is invoiced this month.
  • Running project:
    • actual collections come from the register by their collection dates;
    • invoices not yet collected are forecast after the payment period (one past its period goes in this month, and is listed);
    • the balance of each phase (phase value − submitted) is invoiced as the latest updated schedule earns it: each activity moves from its progress today to complete between its forecast start and finish (or along the original baseline where it is not in the schedule; and along the original baseline from today's progress when no schedule is loaded);

    • an advance received is recovered at the terms' rate.

13. Rules the system enforces

  • Only submitted, reviewed and approved reports count; drafts and rejected reports never reach a dashboard.
  • Reported progress from the weekly updated P6 schedule, including engineering and procurement; daily reports drive manpower, productivity and quantities, and give construction progress until an updated schedule is loaded.
  • Price only from P6; cost only from the Master Manpower Costing Sheet. Each project names which file owns budget manhours.
  • Rates and PO rates are dated; a change never restates a filed report. Before a PO's first rate, the work is costed as an estimate, never at a later rate.
  • One purchase order per subcontractor per project; a second one for the same company is refused.
  • A purchase order with approved or paid cash-out milestones cannot be removed: cancel it in the cash-out forecast instead.
  • Re-baselining moves the plan, never earned progress; every import can be undone.
  • A subcontractor's quantity is entered once per company; all their trades' hours count.
  • Foreman submits, site engineer reviews, site manager approves, as assigned in the approval matrix for that project; the project manager covers anyone unassigned.
  • Every approval emails a summary to the project's stakeholders.
  • Site roles never see cost or billing, and their executive view is set by the super admin.
  • Rejecting or returning needs a comment; every change is logged with a name and time.
  • A lifecycle stage closes only when its owner submits it with every required deliverable ticked and its approver approves it (the Head of Planning and Cost Control for stages 1 to 5, a project manager on the project for stages 6 to 12, or a super admin); nobody approves their own submission; only a super admin reopens a closed stage, with a reason.
  • On the server: a report is saved, reviewed and approved only through ProTrack's server functions. Writing to the tables directly is refused.
  • On the server: a foreman always reports under his own name, can edit only his own drafts, and cannot change a report once submitted.
  • On the server: an approved report cannot be changed or deleted by anyone, and a project that has approved reports cannot be deleted.
  • On the server: report history and the change log can only be added to. Each history entry is sealed with the one before it, so any alteration is detectable.
  • On the server: one report per foreman per day; no future dates; the same activity once per report; no more than 24 hours a day per worker.
  • On the server: loading the same Master Manpower Costing Sheet, invoice register or baseline again changes nothing; a failed row is refused and listed, never half-applied.
  • Every upload is a numbered batch with the file's fingerprint; refused rows must be acknowledged by name before the rest is saved, and a failed upload never changes the last good data.
  • Only Excel, CSV, XER and P6 XML files up to 10 MB are accepted (the updated schedule XER: up to 40 MB); workbooks with macros are refused. BOQ Engine drawings are PDF only, up to 40 MB each by default.

14. FAQ and troubleshooting

We have 100+ projects. Do we create them one by one?

No. As super admin, open Settings → Projects → Import projects from Excel:

  1. Download the template. Its How to fill sheet explains each column, and its Lists sheet holds the regions, sectors, cities, project types and people ProTrackAI knows. Add a missing city or person in Settings first.
  2. Copy your project list into the Projects sheet, one row per project:
    • Always needed: PE number, project name, status (Pre-award, Active, On hold or Completed) and region.
    • Needed unless pre-award: sector, city, start and finish dates.
    • Optional: type, contract value, client and location; the Tender details (TE number, L1 or L2, expected value, notified, NOA and signing dates); and the project manager, site engineer, planning engineer and costing engineer by email or name, who get access.
    • New tenders: leave the PE number empty for a pre-award tender to get the next number.
    • NOA date: also records the finance WBS as released on that date.
  3. Upload it and check the preview. Ready rows are listed, and refused rows show the reason, for example an unknown region or a finish before the start. A person not found is only a warning.
  4. Confirm the refused rows and create the rest. The upload is a numbered batch in the Import centre.

A project whose PE number (or, for a new tender, TE number) is already in ProTrackAI is left unchanged, so you can upload the list again as it grows; the same file twice changes nothing. Download the current project list gives every project in the same layout. On the ProTrack server, the projects, dates, value and team access are saved; the Tender columns stay in the browser until the server is updated.

I cannot see Review or Approve on a report

The approval matrix routes each report to one site engineer and one site manager per project. If it is not assigned to you, the button does not appear. The route is shown at the top of every report; the super admin changes it in Settings → Approval matrix.

A report went to the project manager instead of a site engineer

That foreman, or his site engineer, has no assignment on that project. The project heading in Settings → Approval matrix shows how many are unassigned.

Settings → Email says "Demo: not sent"

Emails are prepared and listed, but the mail function is not set up on the server yet. Follow EMAIL-SETUP.md once.

All my figures look out of date

The app may be in demo mode, which measures everything at 22 Sept 2026. Settings → System shows the mode; switch to live for today's date.

The timer shows zero or stops

It pauses while the app is in the background and resets if you leave the form without saving. Save as draft to keep the time.

Productivity reads very high

A subcontractor quantity is entered without their workers and hours. The form warns in amber.

DCMA shows float as not assessed

The XER was exported without scheduling. Press F9 in P6, then export again.

A project shows zero everywhere

No baseline yet, or the Master Manpower Costing Sheet and labour loading are both missing, so there is no planned rate.

Invoiced is higher than invoiceable

Either invoicing is ahead of certified progress, or the payment terms or price weighting are not set. Check the terms on the project.

A subcontractor line says "estimated"

That company has no purchase order on the project, or no rate for that activity or trade, or the report date is before the effective date of its first rate. The project's costing engineer adds it in Materials & Procurement → Subcontract purchase orders.

"Invalid API key" at sign-in

The key in config.js is incomplete or from another project. Copy the anon key again with the copy icon.

"The server refused this" when reviewing or approving

The server checks the approval matrix itself. Only the site engineer assigned to that foreman on that project can review, and only the site manager assigned to that engineer can approve; the project manager can do both. Ask the super admin to check Settings → Approval matrix.

"This foreman already has a report for that date"

Open the existing report and edit it instead of creating a second one.

"This is the same file as the last one loaded, so nothing changed"

The server recognised the file. If you meant to load corrected figures, check you chose the corrected file.

Undo import or Remove baseline says it is not available

On the server a baseline is never removed, so report history always keeps its activities. Import the correct file again; activities are matched by ID and updated.

An amber bar says the server data could not be loaded

Your connection or the server was unavailable. Press Try again. Anything you change is still sent to the server, which checks it; nothing is saved only in the browser.

I cannot sign in with a demo account

Demo accounts are switched off on a site connected to the server. Use your work email; the super admin creates accounts in Supabase. For a demonstration, open the demo page (demo.html, linked from the sign-in page), where the demo accounts work on sample data.

I cannot approve a lifecycle stage

Stages 1 to 5: only the Head of Planning and Cost Control or a super admin. Stages 6 to 12: only a project manager on that project or a super admin. Never the person who submitted it: if you submitted it, ask another approver.

"Required deliverables are not done"

Tick every deliverable marked Required, or ask the stage's approver (the Head for stages 1 to 5, a project manager for 6 to 12) to mark it Not applicable if it does not apply.

A deliverable shows evidence but is not ticked

That is deliberate: evidence shows what ProTrackAI holds; only the owner confirms a deliverable is done.

The Apply button stays grey

Some rows were refused. Read the reasons (or download the exception report), then tick the box confirming you want to load only the accepted rows. Or fix the file and upload it again.

"The server refused some rows"

The server checks every row again. If it refuses a row the app accepted, the preview shows its message; tick the box to load the rest, or fix the file.

An upload shows as Failed in the Import centre

Nothing from it was saved and your previous data is unchanged. Open the batch for the reason, fix the cause, and upload the same file again: it shows as a retry of the failed attempt.

Delete or undo did nothing

You are on an old version. Upload the latest index.html and press Ctrl+F5.
