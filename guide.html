ProTrack user guide

Construction productivity intelligence: from the daily site report to management decisions.

ProTrack turns what the site records every evening into productivity, earned value, forecast cost and early warnings. The idea behind it is one sentence: quantity alone is progress; quantity together with the resources that produced it is productivity. Everything in the system follows from capturing both, once, at site.

What it covers

Daily progressQuantities, own crews, rental manpower, subcontractors and equipment, captured per activity in under a minute.
Weekly engineering and procurementDesign and long-lead items updated by milestone from one Excel file.
Productivity and earned valueProductivity index, SPI, CPI, EAC, S-curves against original, revised and recovery baselines.
Reports and exportsEleven standard reports in Excel and PDF, including quantity reconciliation, unit rate analysis and a manpower forecast, plus a progress file for Primavera P6.
Early warningsThreshold breaches with a recommended action, before the monthly report tells you.
ProTrack AIPlain-language questions answered from the project's own data.
This is a prototypeData lives in your browser, not on a server. Use it for demonstration, user feedback and process agreement. Do not put real staff passwords or commercially sensitive contract data into it until the production build with a proper backend and authentication service is in place.

2. Getting started

Signing in

  1. Open the ProTrack link. The sign-in page lists demo accounts; tap one to fill the form, or type your own username and password.
  2. Use the eye button to check the password before signing in.
  3. Tick Remember me for a 30-day session on a trusted device. Leave it off on a shared computer and the session ends when you close the tab.
  4. Forgotten passwords go through Forgot password, which issues a one-time link valid for 30 minutes.

Five failed attempts lock sign-in for 60 seconds, eight for 15 minutes. Sessions end automatically after 20 minutes of inactivity, and after 12 hours in any case. Sign out is at the bottom of the sidebar, or in the notifications panel on a phone.

Three controls at the top of every page decide what the numbers cover:

  • Scope — the whole portfolio, one region, or one project.
  • Sector — Infrastructure, Power, Oil & Gas, MEP, Water and any sector you add.

4. Setting up a project

Settings is open to the Super Admin only, at Settings in the sidebar. Work in this order.

  1. Sectors, regions and cities. Add anything missing. A region can have a regional manager; cities belong to a region. Items in use cannot be deleted.
  2. Trades and cost rates. See below — a project cannot be costed correctly without them.
  3. Projects. Create the project with its code, name, short name, sector, region, city, dates, contract value, status and project type (Building, Substation, OHTL, Pipeline, Data centre, Industrial, Other).
  4. Users and access. Create the project manager, site engineers and foremen, and tick the projects each person may see.
  5. Project team. Reopen the project and choose the project manager, site engineer and foremen from those users.
  6. Milestones and EOT. Add project start, intermediate milestones, TCC, PAC or FAC with planned, forecast and actual dates; record any extension of time claim.
  7. Baseline. Import the schedule (next chapter).
  8. Subcontractors and purchase orders. Add the vendors working on the project and their PO rates.

Trades and cost rates

A trade is a labour category: Mason, Carpenter, Electrician, Scaffolder, anything you need. Each trade carries a dated rate history, entered as what you actually pay:

Cost rate per hour = basic monthly salary ÷ working hours per month × loaded cost factor

Hours per month defaults to 208 (26 days × 8 hours). The loaded factor, 2.5 by default, covers accommodation, transport, insurance, mobilisation and overheads. A carpenter on SAR 1,500 basic works out at SAR 18.03 per hour.

Rate changes never rewrite historyWhen the salary rises to SAR 1,800, add a new rate with its effective date. Reports dated before that date keep the old rate; reports from that date use the new one. You can also enter a rate in advance and it takes effect on its own date.

Subcontractors and purchase orders

A subcontractor holds one purchase order per project, so the same company can work on several projects at different rates. Each PO has a commercial basis:

BasisRates are set perExecuted cost
MeasuredActivity, with its unitQuantity executed × PO unit rate
Manpower supply (rental)Trade, per manhourWorkers × hours × PO manhour rate

PO rates are dated in the same way as salaries. If a daily report references a vendor with no PO on that project, the line is costed as an estimate and clearly labelled, so the gap is visible rather than silently wrong.

5. Baselines and the P6 import

Open Baselines, choose the project and the baseline set, then load the file. Planning engineers and the Super Admin can do this.

Three baseline sets

SetPurposeEffect
OriginalThe contract planAll reporting measures against it by default; it creates and updates the activity list
RevisedAfter an approved extension or variationStored as dates alongside the original; selected through "Measure against"
RecoveryA catch-up planSame as revised, usually not contractual
Re-baselining never changes earned progressRevised and recovery sets hold dates only. Budgets, quantities and planned productivity stay with the original, so re-baselining cannot make a slow crew look fast. Activities missing from a revised file keep their original dates.

Accepted files

  • XER — a standard Primavera P6 export. Reads the WBS tree, activity IDs and names, baseline dates, budgeted labour units and physical percent complete.
  • XML (PMXML) — P6 XML export.
  • Excel — the ProTrack template, which you can download from the same screen.

Before importing you choose which WBS level becomes the work location, the default work type, a minimum budget manhours filter, a maximum number of activities, and whether to skip completed activities and milestones. The preview shows how many activities passed, the work locations and work types found, and ten sample rows.

P6 carries manhours, not quantitiesWithout a quantity column, activities are imported in earned manhours: budget quantity equals budgeted hours and the unit is "mh". To track physical quantities (m³, tonnes, metres), add quantity and unit columns using the Excel template.

Activity classes

Every activity is Construction, Engineering or Procurement. Construction is updated only by daily report; engineering and procurement only by the weekly Excel. The import classifies activities automatically from their names and WBS, and you can correct the class when editing an activity.

Each project weights the three classes for its overall progress; the default is construction 60%, procurement 30%, engineering 10%.

6. Daily progress reporting

Site engineers and foremen use New report. One report covers everything a foreman's crews did that day.

  1. Date and foreman. The project is filled in from your access; if you hold more than one project you choose it.
  2. Work location — the area of the site, for example 380kV GIS Building or Zone B.
  3. Work type — Civil, Electrical, Mechanical, Instrumentation or Architectural. Only the types present in that location are offered.
  4. WBS — only the WBS elements under that location and type.
  5. Activity — now a short list instead of the whole baseline. Selecting it fills in the planned quantity for the day and the usual crew.
  6. Quantity executed today, in the activity's unit.
  7. Own labour — trade, number of workers, regular hours and overtime hours.
  8. Subcontractor crews — company, trade, workers, hours, and the quantity they executed. That quantity is part of the total above and is what measures their performance.
  9. Equipment — type, units and working hours.
  10. Add another activity as many times as needed; each has its own location, type, WBS, quantity and resources.
  11. Evidence and remarks — photographs, drawings, and a note about delays, weather, access or instructions. Remarks are read by managers and appear in the project's bottlenecks list.
  12. Submit for approval, or Save draft to finish later.

The panel on the right updates as you type: productivity index for the report and for each activity, manhours, overtime share, labour cost, and the total resources on the report.

Workers on two activitiesRecord them on each activity with the hours spent there, for example 4 hours and 4 hours. The resource summary can therefore be higher than the gate count, which is expected.

The system refuses a report with a quantity but no crew, a crew but no quantity, a future date, or the same activity twice. Each error names the activity.

7. Review and approval

Reports move: Draft → Submitted → Reviewed → Approved, or to Rejected. A returned report goes back to Draft with the manager's comment.

RoleActions available
ForemanSubmit or edit their own draft
Site EngineerSubmit drafts; edit a report until it is approved
Project Manager, Super AdminMark reviewed, approve, reject, or return with comments

Opening a report shows the quantity, every crew and machine, the attachments, the remarks, and a full audit trail of who did what and when. Rejecting or returning requires a comment. Only submitted, reviewed and approved reports feed the dashboards; drafts and rejected reports do not.

Submitting a report notifies the project manager, and raises a separate warning for any activity below 90% productivity.

8. Weekly design and procurement update

Design and procurement have no daily quantities, so planning engineers update them weekly — or whenever needed — from one Excel file, at Weekly update.

  1. Download update template. It contains only the engineering and procurement activities in your scope, with their current status and baseline dates.
  2. Fill the shaded columns: data date, actual start, milestone reached, forecast finish, actual finish, remarks.
  3. Upload filled template. Every row is validated and previewed before anything is saved.
  4. Apply the good rows. Rejected rows can be fixed and uploaded again.

Milestone weights

EngineeringStarted 10% · IDC issued 40% · Comments incorporated 70% · IFC issued 100%
ProcurementPR issued 5% · PO placed 15% · Vendor drawings approved 30% · Manufacturing complete 65% · FAT released 80% · Shipped 90% · Delivered to site 100%

Progress is never typed as a percentage: you enter the milestone and the system sets the figure. Engineering rolls up weighted by budgeted manhours, procurement by package value.

What the upload rejects

  • Unknown activity IDs, or activities outside your project access
  • Construction activities (they belong to the daily report)
  • Milestones that do not exist for that class
  • Dates after the data date, a finish before a start, or an actual finish without the final milestone
  • Progress going backwards with no remark explaining it

Warnings, which do not block the upload, cover items complete without an actual finish, progress without a start date, and missing forecast finish dates. Each upload is stored as a dated batch in the update history with the uploader's name.

9. Dashboards

The Dashboard is the operational view for everyone with project access.

  • Productivity index, last 7 days, with a 30-day trend line and an index ruler showing every project and activity in scope against the 90% and 100% marks.
  • Workforce on site and manhours over 30 days, each with a sparkline, plus earned manhours and overtime share.
  • Schedule (SPI) and Cost (CPI) gauges, and portfolio complete against plan.
  • Early warnings with severity counts and the three most urgent.
  • Portfolio by region and by sector, each with a progress bar of plan against actual. Tap a row to filter the whole site.
  • Daily productivity and manhours chart, and project health with forecast finish and a risk score.
  • Activity performance and the foreman ranking for the last 30 days.

Traffic lights are consistent everywhere: green above 100%, amber 90 to 100%, red below 90%.

10. Executive summary

Open to executives, portfolio and regional managers, planning engineers, project managers and site engineers — each within their own access.

Summary tab

  • Projects, regions, sectors and contract value; portfolio complete against plan; SPI; CPI; productivity; and how many projects need attention.
  • Projects by region and by sector: count, value, progress, SPI, CPI, productivity and critical count.
  • Critical projects, worst first, with Plan %, Actual %, Variance, CPI, SPI, productivity index and the main issue in plain words. A project is flagged when SPI or CPI is under 0.95, productivity is under 90%, or the forecast finish is more than 14 days late.

Project tab

Search by project code, name or sector, then open it for:

  • Progress against plan, SPI with forecast finish and slip, CPI with EAC and VAC, productivity with workforce, earned value figures, and the engineering and procurement position
  • Milestones and completion: planned, forecast and actual dates for project start, intermediate milestones, TCC, PAC and FAC, with the EOT status, days claimed and reference
  • Transmission line key activities for OHTL projects: plan and profile, soil investigation, access road, soil investigation support, foundation design, foundation works, tower delivery, tower erection and stringing, each with scope, done, plan %, actual % and finish date
  • The progress S-curve, bottlenecks and challenges, underperforming activities, and foreman performance

11. Earned value and EAC

The Earned value page reports in the language clients use: planned value, earned value, actual cost, the two performance indices, estimate at completion and variance at completion, with an S-curve and a per-activity table.

TermMeaning in ProTrack
BACBaseline manhours at the blended rate, plus weighted engineering and procurement budgets
PVBAC × planned percent to date, from the selected baseline's dates
EVBAC × actual percent: approved quantities for construction, milestone percent for design and procurement
ACOwn labour at dated trade rates, plus subcontractor cost at PO rates

CPI is calculated from construction only, because design and procurement have no manhour or invoice feed in the prototype. SPI and percent complete include all three classes.

Report controls

Some reports carry their own controls above the table. The manpower histogram has two:

  • Trade — all trades, or a single trade such as electrician or rigger.
  • Period — daily, or weekly averages for a smoother picture.

Whatever you select is what exports, and it is named in the report notes so a printed copy explains itself.

Manpower histogram in detail

The report covers 30 days back and six weeks ahead, with three series:

SeriesSourceAnswers
Planned (dashed line)Baseline daily quantity ÷ planned rate ÷ 8 hours, split across trades by the activity's planned crew mixWhat the schedule assumed
Actual (bars)Headcount in approved and submitted daily reports, own crews and subcontractorsWho actually turned up
Forecast (solid line, future only)Planned headcount × (planned rate ÷ actual rate of the last 21 days)How many workers are needed to hold the baseline at today's productivity

The by-trade table gives planned and actual averages for the last 30 days, the variance, the forecast average and peak for the next six weeks, and to mobilise: the gap between the forecast and the current crew. That column is what a project manager takes to the manpower meeting.

Quantity reconciliation in detail

Per activity: original scope, carried in at go-live, executed by own manpower, by rental manpower, by measured subcontract, total executed, percent and remaining. Own manpower is the daily report quantity less what the subcontractor crews claimed, so each row reconciles two ways:

carried in + own + rental + subcontract = executed  ·  executed + remaining = scope

Quantities are in each activity's own unit, so the columns add across a row and not down the page.

Using the unit rate analysisThe actual cost per unit is your revised rate for estimating similar work. The forecast column tells you what the remaining quantity will cost at that rate rather than at the original one.

Early warning thresholds

  • SPI or CPI below 0.95 (high below 0.90)
  • Forecast finish more than 14 days late (high above 30 days)
  • Activity productivity below 90% over 14 days (high below 80%)
  • Weekly productivity falling more than 5 points
  • Manpower below 85% of plan over 7 days
  • Overtime above 12% of manhours
  • A missing daily report on the last working day
  • Subcontractor productivity below 90%

13. Sending progress back to P6

Open Reports → P6 progress update, set Scope to one project, then download as XER, P6 XML or Excel for P6. The file carries, per started activity: physical percent complete, actual start, actual finish, expected finish, remaining duration in working days, and actual and remaining labour units.

Before loading into P6Set the activity percent complete type to Physical — ProTrack's percent is earned value over budget, not duration — and set the project data date to match the file. Test on a copy of the schedule first; the Excel route is the most predictable because you map the columns yourself.

14. ProTrack AI

The assistant opens from the avatar at the bottom right, and answers for the scope you have selected. Suggested questions are answered instantly from the data; anything you type yourself is answered by Claude using the same project data.

Questions it handles well

  • What is today's productivity?
  • Which activity is underperforming, and by how much?
  • What manpower is needed to finish on time?
  • Show the early warnings; show the EAC forecast.
  • Compare foreman productivity; which subcontractor performed best?
  • Explain the productivity variance on an activity.

Answers quote the activity, the current rate, the crew size required and the dates. It does not invent numbers: if the data does not cover a question, it says so and points at the report that would.

15. Formula reference

Productivity

Actual rate = quantity executed ÷ manhours consumed
Planned rate = budget quantity ÷ budget manhours
Productivity index = actual rate ÷ planned rate — equivalently earned manhours ÷ actual manhours
Earned manhours = quantity executed ÷ planned rate

Green above 100%, amber 90 to 100%, red below 90%. A report covering several activities is weighted by manhours.

Earned value

SPI = EV ÷ PV  ·  CPI = EV ÷ AC  ·  SV = EV − PV  ·  CV = EV − AC
EAC (CPI) = BAC ÷ CPI  ·  EAC (CPI × SPI) = AC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI)
ETC = EAC − AC  ·  VAC = BAC − EAC  ·  TCPI = (BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC)

Cost

Own labour = workers × (regular hours + 1.5 × overtime hours) × trade rate on the report date
Rental manpower = workers × hours × PO manhour rate
Measured subcontract = quantity executed × PO unit rate

Unit rates

Original manhours per unit = budget manhours ÷ budget quantity
Actual manhours per unit = manhours consumed ÷ quantity executed by own and rental crews
Actual cost per unit = total executed cost ÷ quantity executed, split into own, rental and subcontract
Forecast cost to complete = remaining quantity × actual cost per unit

Measured subcontract quantities carry no manhours on your books, so they appear in the cost rates but not the manhour rates.

Planning

Planned headcount = baseline daily quantity ÷ planned rate ÷ 8 hours
Crew needed to recover = remaining quantity ÷ remaining working days ÷ current rate ÷ 9 hours
Forecast finish = today + (BAC − EV) ÷ recent daily earned value
Forecast headcount = planned headcount × (planned rate ÷ actual rate over the last 21 days)
To mobilise = forecast average headcount − current average headcount, per trade

16. Rules the system enforces

  • Only submitted, reviewed and approved reports count. Drafts and rejected reports never reach a dashboard.
  • Construction progress comes only from daily reports; engineering and procurement only from the weekly update. Neither can overwrite the other.
  • Design and procurement progress is set by milestone, never typed as a percentage.
  • Rates are dated. Changing a salary or a PO rate never restates a report already filed.
  • Re-baselining moves the plan, never the earned progress.
  • The original baseline is the default for every report; measuring against another is a deliberate choice and is stated on screen.
  • Rejecting or returning a report requires a comment, and every action is written to the audit trail.
  • Master data in use cannot be deleted; users are deactivated rather than removed.
  • Each subcontractor holds one purchase order per project; a missing PO shows as an estimate rather than a silent zero.

17. Running a pilot

The fastest way to prove the system is one live project for one month.

  1. Week 0 — setup. Load the real P6 baseline, agree the work locations and work types with the site team, set the trade rates from payroll, and load the subcontractor POs.
  2. Week 0 — access. Create the project manager, site engineers, foremen and planning engineer, and walk each through their own screen once.
  3. Weeks 1 to 4 — run it in parallel. Keep the existing Excel DPR for one week as a safety net, then stop it. Agree that approval happens the same evening.
  4. Weekly. The planning engineer uploads the design and procurement update every Sunday, and exports the P6 progress file the same day.
  5. Month end. Compare the ProTrack reports with the manual monthly report, and review the unit rate analysis to see which activities need a revised estimate.

What to decide before going live

  • The critical thresholds (0.95 for SPI and CPI by default; some clients use 0.90)
  • Class weightings per project (60 / 30 / 10 by default)
  • The loaded cost factor for trade rates, agreed with finance
  • Whether office staff sign in with company Microsoft accounts (single sign-on) while site staff use email and password

18. FAQ and troubleshooting

An activity is missing from the daily report

Check three things: it is a Construction class activity, its work location and work type match what you selected, and it belongs to the project you have access to. Engineering and procurement activities never appear here.

The productivity index looks wrong

Compare the quantity and the manhours on the report. The most common cause is a crew recorded on the wrong activity, or a subcontractor quantity recorded without their crew.

The cost of an old report changed

It should not. Rates are dated; if a figure moved, check whether someone entered a new rate with a past effective date.

A subcontractor line says "estimated"

There is no purchase order for that vendor on that project, or no rate for that activity or trade. Add it in Settings → Subcontractors.

SPI moved but nothing was built

Check the "Measure against" selector at the top. You may be looking at the revised or recovery baseline.

A download did not appear

Excel and PDF exports need an internet connection the first time, because the file libraries load on demand. Confirm the download, then retry.

I was signed out

Sessions end after 20 minutes of inactivity, and after 12 hours in any case. Tick "Remember me" on your own device.

Someone left the company

Settings → Users and access → Edit → set Inactive. Their past reports and approvals stay intact.
